## Chronological Storyline

In a seemingly normal day in the world, a story broke out from a remote island that shock the world. Fossils and corpses started to surface from the seas around that remote island. And a hematite mine found a new ore they named "Quietus Ore". A red ore that grew among the hematite deposits.

After a few years of mining, they found weird fossils deep within the mountain. They uncovered strange fossils of a creature that were never recorded in any encyclopedia. An archeologist by the name of XXX were tasked to investigate the fossils that were found at that mountain along with a group of miners to dug deeper into the mines.

From the knowledge that he have, he warns the institute about going deeper to the mines. He once read that there was a record of a folklore about creatures that roam in the caverns of those mines. Judging the freshness of the bones, he worries that there might be some of those creature still exists within the mountain.

With that warning, the institute still insists on doing an expedition and explore the mountain thoroughly. They sailed to the island once everything prepared.

When approacing the island they starts to notice lots of weird fish and huge skulls bones, and spikes all around the water.

After they arrived at the island they set up an outpost near the entrance of the mine and began studying what the miner have found. They also set up some archeology equipment to study the strange fossils they found in the cavern.

After days of investigating, the miner team made a breakthrough, they found a cave entrance that leads to a cave system under the mountain.

At first the archeology team ask for a new team of explorers to help them explore the caves and kill any monsters in that cave, but the institute pressured them to explore slowly with caution, while the explorer team is sailing to that remote island.

Following the institute orders to proceed deeper into the mountain with caution, they set up various outposts along the way in the deep caverns, mainly in areas that needed attention the most. They start with making an outpost near the entrance of the deep cavern, "Outpost Alpha". From there they explored and studied the deep cavern thoroughly.

The more they explore, the more they found weird fossils of an unknown origin inside the cave. Not long after they explore the cave, they found strange red matter along with bone spikes inside the cave. They hypothesised that the red matter could be related to the ore they named "Quietus" in the mountains. Shortly after this discovery, the team set up another camp to study this named "Camp Bravo".

As they venture deep inside the cavern, the archeology team starts to feel weird about them. They started to see vision and dreams of some beings incomprehensible to human mind. And they started to feel an unknown pull towards the center of the mountain.

After a few more days of exploring, they followed the red matter deeper and set up their last outpost, "Outpost Charlie". There they confirmed their suspicion, the red matter was seeping into the hematite-rich rock around it and turning it into Quietus ore.

At Outpost Charlie, every member of the team felt the same thing, a sense, at once ominous and welcoming, that something was waiting for them further down. After a group discussion, they agreed to stay put and wait for the explorer team, which was due to arrive within a few days. But the explorers were delayed, the days stretched into a couple of weeks, and curiosity eventually got the better of them. They went deeper.

On the way to the deepest part of the cavern, the whispers and visions grew stronger.

At one point, one of the team members collapsed. The others stopped to help, but he insisted that they keep going and leave him behind. Two more archeologists, too ill to go on, stayed behind as well, leaving only three archeologists, the professor among them, to continue the descent. They never knew what caused the illnes, but days before this, the three of them already starting to feel it, but they thought that the illness feeling is just because they were overworked. Suspiciously, the three of them were the people who investigated the arachnid fossils and the hands-on researcher regarding the red matter.

As they neared the end of the path, madness slowly took hold of them. The professor sensed it too, though he seemed to retain his conciousness. The two researchers walking beside him however, had lost theirs conciousness. They moved like sleepwalkers, their gazes fixed straight ahead, as if being drawn into the very heart of the mountain. The professor kept his observation to himself and remained silent, telling himself that it might be just a hallucination.

As they enter the final chamber of the deep cavern, they were met with an unfathomable sight. The skull of a colossal, unknown creature, a view the professor describe in his notes as "eldritch". The chamber was laced with a red substance, spikes comming out from the walls, and massive, eye-like structures hung from the ceeiling. At first, they thought the eyes were alive, yet none of them ever moved.

The professor instructed the two researchers to measure the hematite concentration in the chamber while he examined the skull. As he jotted down his notes, the pair ceased stopped doing work and approached him. He asked if they needed anything, but neither replied. They simply walked past him, heading straight toward the skull. He tried to stop them, but they pressed onward, as if they had been subjected to brainwashing.

Suddenly, a scream came out from the entrance of the chamber. The professor made one last attempt to reach for the two researchers, but his hand slipped away. He ran to a small cave near the floor of the chamber to hide. From his concealment, he watched as the bodies of the two researchers contorted and stretched, transforming into tall, alien-like creatures. Shortly thereafter, three spider-like beings crawled in through the entrance. presumably the three archaeologists who had been left behind due to illness. All five transformed archaeologists let out cries directed toward the skull, as if in prayer. Then, the three spider-like creatures left the chamber and headed up to the mine on the surface, where they attacked the miners.

The professor remained in his hiding place, recording everything he had witnessed, too terrified to emerge. Days passed, and his own willpower began to fade, until he, too, had all but lost his sanity, much like the others. His final note is an apology for failing to prevent all of this. It concludes with him ominously saying that he is now setting off to join his creator. He then left his hiding spot. The two tall beings spotted him immediately, yet he made no attempt to flee. Instead, he turned toward the skull and began to pray, just as the others had done—before the two creatures disemboweled him.

#### Notes on the Marine Remains

Author: Ellen Carrow
Title: Notes on the Marine Remains
Type: Field Notebook
Location: Dragon Skull (the one in the sea)

```txt
FIELD NOTEBOOK
E. Carrow
Marine remains, north shallows

5 Sept. Rowed out with Forth at the ebb. The great skull stands with its jaw in the sand and its crown some 30 ft out of the water. Not whale. Not any reptile I know of. Bone pale and very dense, with rings like a tree's under the lens, too many to count.

M-1. Part of a limb, from the sand under the jaw. 4 ft 2 in. A joint at each end. Outer surface plated, not bony. Stiff hairs down one edge, a quarter-inch long.
M-2 to M-7. The same, smaller.

The fishermen say these first washed up three summers ago, and before then the shallows were bare sand. I take that for a fisherman's tale. Still, the hairs. Nothing which has lain an age in salt water has its hairs still.

6 Sept. Forth made a count of the spikes round the skull: 41. He is sure they are arranged. I am sure they are not. We have agreed to measure the spacing and argue later.

8 Sept. Measured. He is right. Spaced true to within two inches.

8 Sept, evening. The limbs were limp as rope when we took them up from the sand. In the crate they have stiffened again, and two of them have drawn up at the joints. My fingers are numb from the sea; it has been cold all week.

The professor wants all marine work stopped and all of us in the mine. I will leave this book here and come again for the rest of the limbs.
```

#### Notes on the Carvings at the Upper Skull

Author: Albert Wilmot
Title: Notes on the Carvings at the Upper Skull
Type: Scholar's Notes (translation)
Location: Dragon Skull (the one above the mountain)

```txt
NOTES ON THE CARVINGS AT THE UPPER SKULL
A. Wilmot, folklorist to the relief party
20 Oct.

Dr. Moore has told the relief party to keep to the mine. I have climbed to this skull instead, for no one in the town will come near it and I wished to know why.

The brow is carved in marks grouped by fives. I have seen the system before in scraps, but never so much of it. What follows is my best rendering. Words in brackets are guesses, and I am not proud of all of them.

This is a door. It stands [shut].
Beyond it lies the [place-before], as it was in its own day, forty thousand thousand years before the hill.
The door does not open to the hand. It opens to the [key], which is carried here and not made here.
Who enters bearing the key walks in the before-time, and may return.
Who comes without the key does not enter.
Take nothing living out of the before-time.

The last line is cut twice as deep as the rest.

The figure in the second line is either forty million years or an error of mine. I should much prefer the error.

Mrs. Pym at the harbor inn tells me there is a man on this island who keeps such a key and will let a traveller pass for a price. I have inquired after him in the town. No one will say where he is. I am told if he wants you, he finds you.
```

#### The Warning

Author: Prof. Nathaniel Dwyer
Title: The Warning
Type: Letter
Location: Surface Mine

```txt
Starkweather Institute
Department of Palaeontology
11 June

To the Board,

I am forced to put my thoughts in writing, for the Board declined my advice without asking me why I gave it. It is altogether against my will that I set out my reasons for opposing this expedition they have tasked me with, and I am the more reluctant as I suspected my warning will be in vain.

The eleven specimens which were sent to us from the island's hematite mine came out of what the foreman calls the first fossil pit, some 90 ft below the mine floor. I have examined them all. I am a palaeontologist at a modest institution and no authority on living tissue,
but I can say this much with confidence. Some of the bones are not relatively old. The marrow channels within the bones are still open. One of the fragment, when I cut it, was damp.

A bone that is damp did not came from a creature which died before the iron materialized. It came from a creature which died semi-recently, and that creature may have left their relatives behind.

There is a second circumstance, and I know how it will sound. Some years ago, I was unwise enough to read that in our own archive, there is a copied leaf of island folklore which speaks of creatures which walk the caverns under that mountain,
and of bones there which "are not really relatively old". I did not believe it then. I do not say that I believe it now. But rather, I say only that the bones on my table agree with it, and I would very much rather they did not at all.

I ask that the expedition keep to the upper workings, and that no one go below the fossil pits till we know what left them.

Your servant,
N. Dwyer
Professor of Palaeontology

[Stamped across the foot of the page in red ink:]
RECEIVED 19 JUNE. REQUEST DECLINED. EXPEDITION PROCEEDS AS PLANNED. PROF. DWYER TO LEAD.
```

```txt
Hematite workings, lower drifts
Foreman: M. Thorfinn

10 Sept. They want the second pocket now, lower down in Drift 4. Told them it is tight. They went all the same. Gedding and Carrow have moved their tables down there.
```

#### Specimen Notes, Second Pit

Author: Walter Gedding
Title: Specimen Notes, Second Pit
Type: Specimen Notes
Location: Second Fossil

```txt
SPECIMEN NOTES
Second pit, Drift 4
W. Gedding

No. 21. Head plate, partial. 2 ft 9 in wide. Eight shallow pits in two rows. Eye sockets, I am almost sure. Carrow said eight was a great many eyes. I said it was the usual number, for a spider. We did not talk much the rest of the day.

No. 22. Leg, nine-jointed, whole. 11 ft 4 in laid out on the floor of the drift. The joint surfaces are smooth, all but polished, as if from use.

No. 23. Leg, as No. 22. Its mirror.

Nos. 24 to 37. Legs, plates, and one mandible with the tip sharp enough yet to cut my glove. I do not think it got to the hand.

The miners call these the Old Tenants, on the grounds that they were here first. The name has crept into my book and I have given up striking it out.

13 Sept. Some pieces show red in the joints, the same red as the Quietus seam higher in the wall. Lakely wants a sample. Lakely will want a sample of the drift floor next.

None of this bone is mineralised. I have written that line three times now and read it each time, as if it might change.

14 Sept. Hands numb again this morning, both of them, to the second knuckle. Overwork, no doubt, and the cold. Carrow has the same complaint, so I am not uneasy.

We all go down by way of the miners' breakthrough tomorrow, into the big cave at the north end of Drift 4. Nos. 21 to 37 are packed and under the table for collection.
```

```txt
Your loving husband,
Tom
```

```txt
We came down by the miners' breakthrough at 0800, all six of the party, with eleven loads of stores and the last six canaries. The descent is 212 ft by Peabody's line, the last forty of it loose scree, on which Lakely fell twice and grumbled at a length out of all proportion to his injuries.

The cavern below is larger than I had any right to expect. Our lamps do not find the roof. Peabody, timing the echo of a dropped hammer, makes it something over 300 ft, and I have no better figure to offer. The walls are hematite, banded red and grey, and under the lamps they put me in mind of old Roerick's canvases in the harbor inn: the mountain painted from below, by a man who has never stood under it and seems to know it all the same.

We have named this camp Outpost Alpha and pitched the tents against the east wall, out of the draught.

16 Sept.
I have sent to the Institute by wireless for a party of seasoned cavers, armed, to clear the lower passages before we go deeper. The answer was with us before supper. The relief party sails on the 18th, and in the meantime we are to proceed "slowly, and with every caution". I can find nothing in it to disagree with, and I have tried.

18 Sept.
Bone in all the walls. Most of it is of no animal in any catalogue, and a good deal of it has the same jointed form as Gedding's Old Tenants. I am a palaeontologist and accustomed to the dead outnumbering the living. Here they outnumber us by a margin I find difficult to state calmly.

19 Sept.
Forth reports a seam of red ooze on the lower terrace, with white spikes of bone standing out of the rock around it. Lakely went to look and did not return for supper, which means he is happy. We move the laboratory down to the spikes tomorrow, and shall call the place Outpost Bravo.
```

#### Expedition Journal II, Outpost Charlie

Author: Prof. Nathaniel Dwyer
Title: Expedition Journal II
Type: Expedition Journal
Location: Outpost Charlie

```txt
EXPEDITION JOURNAL OF N. DWYER
Outpost Charlie

26 Sept.
Camp set at the foot of the shaft, 410 ft below the mine floor by Peabody's line. The red seam comes down the shaft with us and goes off into a passage to the south, which falls away and which none of us has yet entered.

27 Sept.
Lakely's theory is borne out, and I record it as he would wish, without qualification. Where the red touches the hematite, the hematite becomes Quietus. We saw it pass over a four-inch face in the space of eleven hours. The colour moves into the stone the way damp moves into paper. The ore the miners have dug out of this mountain these many years is no common deposit. Lakely calls it a symptom. I have not found a better word.

28 Sept.
I must now note a circumstance which I would leave out if I thought the record could be honest without it.

All the party, each alone, and before any of us had spoken of it, has confessed, since we came to Charlie, to the same sensation: that something deeper in the south passage awaits us. I get the feeling myself. Its an ominous feeling, but strangely... God help me, welcoming... the way a lit window is welcoming on a cold road. I have offered the party all the natural explanations I have. The depth. The air. Three weeks without daylight. They accepted each in turn, as I did, and none of them has made the feeling any the less.

We have called a meeting for tonight. Forth will take the minutes.

2 Oct.
The relief ship has broken a shaft at sea. Fourteen days at the least.

7 Oct.
Lakely has given up his gloves. I have told him twice.

10 Oct.
I find I spend a good part of each day at the mouth of the south passage with my lamp out, listening. I tell myself it is scientific interest. It is a fact, and I set it down against myself, that curiosity has long since got the better of caution in this camp, and that I am not exempt.
```

```txt
EXPEDITION JOURNAL OF N. DWYER
The south passage. Loose pages.

12 Oct.
Left Charlie at 0600 with six days' stores. The passage falls at about one in six and keeps to the red seam all the way. The seam widens as we descend and is now some two feet wide. The spikes stand thicker along it.

The whispering set in at about the 700 ft mark. I write "whispering" as I have no better word, and as all five of the others used the same word before I did. It is in no language. It does not seem to need one.

At 1430 Lakely fell and could not get up. He has no injury I can find. He refused, with his usual courtesy, which is to say none, to let us carry him. He said he would only slow us, and that the relief party could collect him on their way down. We left him water and Peabody's spare lamp. Poor Lakely. He was laughing when we turned the bend.

13 Oct.
Gedding and Carrow can go no further. Both have numbness to the elbow and a great tiredness, which, I now learn, they have both felt for a fortnight and put down to overwork. When they told me, I put it down to overwork as well.

I set the next part down, for I cannot stop seeing it. The three who have fallen out are Gedding and Carrow, who spent a month with their hands among the Old Tenants, and Lakely, who would not wear his gloves at the red pool. Forth, Peabody and I handled neither half so much. I do not know what that means. I am afraid I know just what it means.

I leave these pages with Gedding for the relief party. Forth, Peabody and I go on to see what lies at the bottom. I will not pretend any longer that I go for the sake of science. The pull of what lies below is stronger in some men than they suspect, and I am sorry to find it strongest in me.
```

#### Expedition Journal IV, From the Hollow

Author: Prof. Nathaniel Dwyer

Type: Expedition Journal
Location: Heart of the Plague, inside small cave the prof hides in

```txt
EXPEDITION JOURNAL OF N. DWYER
Written in a hollow near the floor of the great chamber. 14 Oct, or the 15th.

I lost my field notes in the chamber. I will put the rest in order, for order is the one thing I still have which belongs wholly to me.

Forth and Peabody came to me over the floor and I asked if they needed anything. No one answered. They walked past me, close enough to touch, towards the skull. I grabbed Peabody by the sleeve and he didn't even slow down. I called them both by name. I called you, I'm ashamed to say, in the voice I use when you made a calculation error, as if that could get you back.

Then there came a scream from the entrance behind us, a long scream which rose and fell over a range no throat of ours could cope with.

I reached for Forth one last time. My hand slipped off his coat. I ran, God forgive me, and found this hollow, and from here I looked on.

What befell the two of them in the next hour, I leave open, for their families and for my own. I just want to say that when it was done, they were much taller than any man and that they did not seem to be in pain.

Then three more came in at the entrance, low to the ground, on very many legs. One of them had a piece of institute green in his joints. I knew the coat. I had told his owner twice to wear his gloves.

The five turned to the skull and shouted together, and it was a prayer. I cannot defend this word on any scientific ground and I will not withdraw it.

When the shouting stopped, the three low ones made their way up the aisle, the way we had come, towards the mine. There are men in the upper drifts. God help Thorfinn's team.

Let me be clear, as there is no one left to take care of. Whatever they became, they were my students... Lakely, who could not leave a rehearsal alone. Gedding and Carrow, who would argue a measurement for an hour and then go and take it again. Peabody, who was carrying the heavy backpack. And poor Forth, who saw it all first and told me about it each morning at breakfast, while I was buttering my bread.
```

#### Final Entry

Author: Prof. Nathaniel Dwyer
Title: Final Entry
Type: Letter
Location: Heart of the Plague, below the skull with the professor's remains

```txt
To the adventurer party or any soul that venture deep into the deep cavern, if it comes, and to the Board of the Starkweather Institute.

I have been in this deep hollow for days. I cannot ascertain how many. My watch have stopped on the second day that i have been here and I did not care to wind it, which I now see was the first thing I stopped caring about.

I owe all of you an apology, and I rather make it plainly than well. Before this all began, I warned the Board that this mountain should be let alone. Then I led five young bright minded souls into it, that was my mistake, and at the end I went deeper than any of them, for I wanted to. Lakely, Gedding, Carrow, Peabody and Forth are what they are since I did not keep my own advice. Thorfinn and his men, I fear they are what they are for the same reason. I am so sorry... I know it is not enough, but it is all that I have.

If you read this, please, do not come down. Seal this cave. Leave the bones where they lie, and leave the red matter to do what it has been doing to the iron in this mountain, let it be. All of it should be left alone.

The two tall creature have stood at the skull since the others left. They do not eat and they do not sleep. I cant stand to look at them. Not out of terror, but out of guilt... Now and then, they cry out, and since yesterday the cry has changed. It has begun to sound very like "Professor", the way Forth used to call it out over the camp when he had found something. Im sorry... I did not know it would all turn like this...

If I had one wish I could make, I wish we could turn back time. to the good old days. When I must said Forth to sleep, but now he's stretched out. All because of me...

I no longer think the feeling at Charlie was a warning. It was an invitation. Lakely was right to call it one, and Carrow was right to want the word struck out.

They are calling again. The voice in my head is becoming louder and louder.

Maybe... they can fullfill my wish...

I am setting off now... to join my creator...
```
